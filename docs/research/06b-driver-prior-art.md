# Driver prior art: output-to-a-real-device (and DSP) from inside a HAL plug-in

Research date: 2026-09-28. Scope: every project found that either (a) opens an IOProc on a real output device from inside an `AudioServerPlugIn`, which is what "design A" means for `eq`, or (b) does non-trivial DSP or I/O work inside `DoIOOperation`. For each one we asked how it behaves on macOS 14, 15 and 26.

Sources are primary unless marked otherwise: source code (cloned and read), GitHub issues/PRs/fork diffs pulled through `gh`, Apple's SDK header, and vendor knowledge-base pages. The web-search budget ran out partway through, so closed-source products (Boom 3D, FxSound for Mac) could not be researched beyond what is listed here. They are marked "unverified".

## TL;DR

- **Design A works on macOS 26, but no project proves it is robust there yet.** One project runs this exact architecture at scale: **Proxy Audio Device (PAD)**, with a Homebrew cask, MacPorts, 75 forks and seven years of issues. PAD is reported working on 26.4 (PR #71, tested on 26.4; #62 reporter: "Since MacOS 26.4 ... audio did recover"; #75, Sep 2026). Two tiny 2026 projects, **mac-display-volume** (macOS 15+) and the **Spotiglass EQ driver** (a 10-band biquad in `DoIOOperation` that forwards to hardware on macOS 26), copy the pattern. Both are months old and have essentially no users.
- **It is explicitly unsupported by Apple.** The macOS 26.5 SDK header `AudioServerPlugIn.h` says: *"First and foremost, an AudioServerPlugIn may not make any calls to the client HAL API in the CoreAudio.framework. This will result in undefined (but generally bad) behavior."* ([QA1811](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1811/_index.html) says the same.) PAD's author calls it "breaking some rules and doing some hacky things" (#6). He also allows that Apple may have "further restricted what audio device drivers are allowed to do" (#62). Two other 2026 projects, snowball-boost and OpenConnct, rejected design A for exactly this reason and do the device I/O in a companion app.
- **What goes wrong in practice is clocking, not deadlocks.** PAD's seven-year "silent until you restart coreaudiod" bug (#14, #19, #43, #62) was root-caused in Aug 2026 (PR #74, shipped in v1.1.0b1) as two defects:
  1. The target device's IO engine restarts in place on display wake or `usbaudiod` session restart. Its sample time snaps back to about 13 700 with no property notification, so the plug-in's cached input→output delta goes stale.
  2. The virtual device's synthetic clock ran open-loop and drifted by some ppm without bound.

  Neither is macOS-26-specific. Both are inherent to design A, because you own a second clock domain and must slave it to the real one.
- **Survivor rules** (details in the last section):
  - Never call the HAL client API on a HAL thread. Use a dedicated serial queue or thread.
  - Phase-lock the virtual clock to the target.
  - Detect timeline discontinuities on both sides and re-anchor.
  - Keep the real-time path lock-free.
  - Count clients correctly.
  - Keep `GetZeroTimeStamp` sane before `StartIO`.
  - Watchdog the target IOProc and rebuild it.
  - Stop the target IOProc when idle.
  - Re-resolve the device by UID with retries.
- **Rogue Amoeba left the HAL-plug-in route.** ACE shipped as `/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/HAL/ACE.driver` plus two launch daemons and needed a "complex installation process ... on Apple Silicon". It was replaced by the ARK backend "since MacOS 14.4 (Airfoil, Audio Hijack, Piezo) and 14.5 (Loopback, SoundSource)" and is fully deprecated on macOS 15 (RA KB [ACE-Uninstall](https://rogueamoeba.com/support/knowledgebase/?showArticle=ACE-Uninstall), [ACE-Legacy](https://rogueamoeba.com/support/knowledgebase/?showArticle=ACE-Legacy)). Their 2026 posts describe the macOS 26 bugs they hit as aggregate-device and capture bugs, fixed in macOS 27 ([post](https://weblog.rogueamoeba.com/2026/09/24/macos-27-fixes-multiple-audio-bugs/)). That points to taps plus aggregates, not a plug-in, as their current mechanism; the aggregate-device part is inferred, not stated.

## Architectural fact that reframes design A

Third-party plug-ins do **not** run inside `coreaudiod`. They run in a sandboxed helper that coreaudiod spawns, `com.apple.audio.Core-Audio-Driver-Service` (shown as `Core Audio Driver (X.driver)` in logs; see the log excerpts in PAD #14 and #43, the AppFaders lessons doc, and libASPL's "Sandboxing" section). Consequences:

- A plug-in's `AudioDeviceCreateIOProcID`/`AudioDeviceStart` on a real device is an ordinary **HAL client connection over IPC** from that helper. PAD logs show `HALC_ProxyIOContext` activity in the driver-service process. The output IOProc runs on a client IO thread in the helper, not on the hardware engine's thread. Design A therefore still has "a process reading/writing audio". That process is Apple's driver-service helper, which reads no input.
- **Privacy indicator:** an output-only IOProc does not open input, and none of the design-A projects reports an indicator or a TCC prompt. This is inferred from the absence of such reports plus the code: PAD, mac-display-volume and Spotiglass open output-only IOProcs. One strong corroborating datum: PAD #14's log shows the driver-service sandbox **cannot even reach `tccd`** (`activating connection ... name=com.apple.tccd` → `failed to do a bootstrap look-up: xpc_error=[3: No such process]`). Any design that opened *input* from the plug-in would fail TCC rather than prompt, so design A must stay output-only.
- **"Adaptive unboosted daemon":** PAD #43 (Ventura 13.6.4) captured `HALC_ProxyIOContext::_StartIO(): Client running as an adaptive unboosted daemon` immediately before every `AudioDeviceStart` failure (`kAudioHardwareIllegalOperationError`). The HAL applies daemon-client policy to the plug-in's own client connection. The case was never resolved, and nobody has reproduced it since. It is a live risk for design A: the start call can fail transiently, so retry it.
- **Sandbox:** the plug-in may read only its own bundle and system frameworks, and write only to cache/temp dirs. Mach services must be declared in `AudioServerPlugIn_MachServices`, network in `AudioServerPlugIn_Network`, and custom IOKit user clients in `AudioServerPlugIn_IOKitUserClients` (SDK header). Spotiglass still reads coefficients from a `/tmp` file that the app writes, and says this works on macOS 26.
- **Code signing (disputed):** Spotiglass ("coreaudiod on macOS 26 refuses to load `.driver` bundles signed only ad-hoc") and OpenConnct ("ad-hoc signing is rejected by the HAL") claim Developer ID is required. snowball-boost (tested on macOS 27) says ad-hoc "is sufficient to load". PAD PR #74 was built ad-hoc on macOS 15. Treat this as unresolved and test it on the target OS.

## 1. Proxy Audio Device: the reference implementation of design A

Repo: [briankendall/proxy-audio-device](https://github.com/briankendall/proxy-audio-device). Licence: Unlicense (public domain). C++, a fork of Apple's NullAudio. Latest stable is v1.0.7 (2023-11-20, "Sonoma compatible"). v1.1.0b1 (2026-08-29) is a pre-release that carries the clock fixes. The owner no longer uses it daily and says he intends to skip macOS 26 (#62).

### How it works (read from `ProxyAudioDevice.cpp` at `be390f7` and tag `v1.1.0b1`)

| Concern | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Device | One output stream, 2 ch Float32, rates 22.05–192 kHz, software volume/mute controls (the product's whole point is volume keys for fixed-volume DACs). |
| Thread for HAL client calls | `Initialize` creates the serial queue `net.briankendall.ProxyAudioDevice.audioOutputQueue` (QoS `USER_INITIATED`). `initializeOutputDevice()` runs `dispatch_after(1 s)` on that queue, with the comment *"Any initialization that involves calling CoreAudio APIs must be done here in a separate thread from the rest of the driver. Otherwise we'll get deadlocks!"*. Target lookup (`AudioObjectGetPropertyData` over all devices), `AudioDeviceCreateIOProcID`, listeners (`DeviceIsAlive`, `NominalSampleRate`, system `Devices`), `AudioDeviceStart`/`Stop` and `RequestDeviceConfigurationChange` all run there. `StartIO`/`StopIO` only set counters and `dispatch_async` the start/stop decision. |
| Idle start/stop | A 500 ms dispatch timer (`monitorUserActivity`) evaluates `ActiveCondition`: `proxiedDeviceActive` means the target IOProc runs only while a client runs IO on the proxy; `userActive` means it also runs while the user is non-idle (<30 s, via IOKit HID idle time); `always` means it runs permanently. Stopping calls `resetInputData()`. |
| Ring buffer | `AudioRingBuffer`, 88 200 frames, indexed by **absolute sample time**. `DoIOOperation(WriteMix)` stores at `inIOCycleInfo->mOutputTime.mSampleTime`. The target IOProc fetches at `inOutputTime->mSampleTime + inputOutputSampleDelta`. The delta is anchored once so that the read position trails the write position by input buffer + output buffer + safety offset. |
| Clock (`GetZeroTimeStamp`) | Synthetic: anchor `mach_absolute_time()` at first `StartIO`, period `kDevice_RingBufferSize` = 16 384 frames. Host ticks per period are scaled by the mean of the target IOProc's `mRateScalar` samples collected since the last call. v1.0.7 bug: the accumulator was zeroed on *every* call and fell back to 1.0 when no target cycle had run, and it advanced at most one period per call. |
| Sample-rate following | A listener on the target's `NominalSampleRate` stops the target, resets, and calls `host->RequestDeviceConfigurationChange(newRate)` on the queue. `PerformDeviceConfigurationChange` updates the rate and then calls `matchOutputDeviceSampleRate()`, which does a **synchronous `AudioObjectGetPropertyData` on the target from the HAL's configuration-change thread**. That is a rule violation not routed through the queue (our reading; no issue attributes a hang to it). |
| Locking | `CAMutex` (pthread) `IOMutex`, `stateMutex` and `getZeroTimestampMutex` are taken on **both** real-time threads (`DoIOOperation` and the target IOProc) and by `resetInputData()` from control threads. The `marrusl` fork replaces this with SPSC atomics (see forks). |
| Config IPC | The settings app sets the box *name* property to magic strings that the driver parses ("If you want to see a total hack...", #6). Settings persist via `host->WriteToStorage` into `/Library/Preferences/Audio/com.apple.audio.SystemSettings.plist`. |

v1.1.0b1 (from vlasky's PR #74, +299/−91 lines) adds:

- A **PI servo** on the phase error between the proxy timeline and the target IO cycles: `kProxyClockGainP = 1/(64·16384)`, `GainI = P/128`, correction clamped to **±500 ppm**, with a ~24 s time constant and damping ≈ 0.7. Phase is measured by projecting the proxy timeline onto each target cycle's host time, not by ring fill level. PR #74 says the fill-level approach "hunts": ±256-frame errors and ±250 ppm swings.
- **Timeline-discontinuity detection** on both sides. Any backward jump, or a forward jump above 16 384 frames, of the expected `mSampleTime` triggers a re-anchor.
- **Client-count fixes:** reset only on the first `StartIO` and set the final frame only on the last `StopIO`.
- Advancing `GetZeroTimeStamp` by all elapsed periods.

The author measured this over 12 days on macOS 15 (Dell U2723QE over DisplayPort): the original symptom never occurred; re-anchors fired about 5 times a day with audio continuing; mean correction 0.05 ppm, max 1.2 ppm; zero overruns; coreaudiod up 12 days.

## 3. Articles and documentation

- **Apple, `AudioServerPlugIn.h`** (macOS 26.5 SDK, lines 34–57) and **[QA1811](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1811/_index.html)**: separate sandboxed process, no client-HAL-API calls, the Info.plist keys for mach services, IOKit user clients and network. The only Apple statement on design A, and it is a prohibition. The header also restricts `PropertiesChanged()` on devices to changes that don't affect IO or structure. IO-affecting changes must go through `RequestDeviceConfigurationChange` → `PerformDeviceConfigurationChange`, which is what PAD does for rate following.
- **libASPL README** ([gavv/libASPL](https://github.com/gavv/libASPL)), sections "Thread and realtime safety" and "Sandboxing":
  - Realtime-safe means never blocking on a lock that a non-realtime thread can hold.
  - The standard library's hidden locks (allocation, stdio, `shared_ptr` atomics) are not realtime-safe.
  - `DoubleBuffer` (blocking setter, lock-free getter) is the pattern for parameter updates.
  - The sandbox allows IPC, shared memory, semaphores, network and IOKit.

## Engineering rules the survivors follow (and where each comes from)

1. **Never call the HAL client API on a thread the HAL gave you.** No `AudioObjectGetPropertyData`, `AudioDeviceCreateIOProcID`, `Start`/`Stop` or listener registration inside `Initialize`, `StartIO`, `StopIO`, `PerformDeviceConfigurationChange` or property handlers. Hop to your own serial queue or thread and return immediately. (PAD, mac-display-volume, Spotiglass. PAD still violates this in `PerformDeviceConfigurationChange` → `matchOutputDeviceSampleRate`; don't copy that.)
2. **Your device's clock is synthetic: phase-lock it to the target.** Feed forward the target's `mRateScalar`, but only when `kAudioTimeStampRateScalarValid` is set and the value is plausible. Add a clamped PI loop (≤500 ppm) on the phase error, measured by projecting your timeline onto the host time of each target cycle, not on ring fill. Advance `GetZeroTimeStamp` by every elapsed period. (PAD v1.1.0b1.) The FIFO-without-servo designs (Spotiglass, mac-display-volume) will drift into periodic drop/underrun glitches; they mask it with drop-oldest or re-priming.
3. **Detect timeline discontinuities on both sides and re-anchor.** The target's `mSampleTime` resets on display wake, `usbaudiod` session restarts (~every 30 min on vlasky's machine), and format changes by other processes, all with the same `AudioObjectID` and no notification. Any backward jump, or a forward jump larger than one zero-timestamp period, means re-anchor. Do the same check on your own `WriteMix` sample times. (PAD PR #74.)
4. **Keep the real-time path lock-free.** Use an SPSC ring with acquire/release indices. Only the consumer advances `read`. Hand parameters (EQ coefficients) over with a seqlock or double buffer. No pthread mutexes shared with control threads. (marrusl fork, libASPL, Spotiglass.) For router or target swaps, use an RCU-style lease or users-count plus deferred close; Spotiglass #248 is the use-after-free that happens otherwise.
5. **Count clients.** The HAL calls `StartIO`/`StopIO` once per client, with bursts like start/stop/start within 50 ms. Reset state only on 0→1 and finalize only on 1→0. (PAD PR #74.)
6. **Make `GetZeroTimeStamp` sane before the first `StartIO`** (anchor = now if unanchored). Otherwise, on macOS 26.5, coreaudiod reportedly never starts the device. (AudioRouterNow; single-project evidence.)
7. **Watchdog the target IOProc and rebuild it.** If the target callback hasn't run for ~1 s while you have clients, or after `kIOMessageSystemHasPoweredOn`, destroy and re-create the IOProc on the target. (mac-display-volume, pokoblin fork.) Treat `AudioDeviceStart` failure as transient and retry; PAD #43 saw `IllegalOperation` under "adaptive unboosted daemon".
8. **Stop the target IOProc when you are idle.** A permanently running target IOProc keeps the device awake, stops the system from sleeping (PAD #25), and correlates with pops on other devices (PAD #39). Use a short idle delay (mac-display-volume: 2 s) rather than stop/start on every client to avoid ~200 ms warm-up gaps (Spotiglass).
9. **Resolve the target by UID, tolerate UID churn, retry.** Listen to the system `Devices` list, retry when the target is missing at boot (1–5 s), match USB UIDs modulo location ID, and offer "hide when unavailable". (PAD PR #70, PR #71, GuaiYiHu fork.)
10. **Follow the target's sample rate through `RequestDeviceConfigurationChange`**, never by changing your stream format in place. Reset DSP state (biquad z1/z2) and recompute coefficients for the new rate. (PAD; Spotiglass #246.)
11. **Handle arbitrary target layouts:** interleaved or non-interleaved buffers, and more than 2 channels. Consume the ring once per cycle, not once per buffer. (Spotiglass #254.)
12. **Stay output-only.** The driver-service sandbox cannot reach `tccd`, so any input path from the plug-in would fail rather than prompt. Output-only is also what keeps the privacy indicator off.
13. **Crash-proof the plug-in.** A crash in the helper removes the device ("not found": PAD #34), and hangs take down all audio (roc-vad #13). CF ownership bugs and use-after-free during teardown are the observed crash classes.

## Bottom line for `eq`

Design A is viable, and one mature open-source project (PAD) runs it on macOS 15 and 26.4. But:

- Apple's header forbids it.
- The only mature implementation needed seven years and an outside contributor to fix its clocking.
- There is exactly one report of macOS 26 *improving* its behaviour (26.4) and zero of an EQ running this way with real users.
- The biggest audio-software vendor on the platform moved off HAL-plug-in capture after macOS 14.4.

If `eq` builds design A, copy PAD v1.1.0b1's clock and discontinuity logic plus the marrusl fork's lock-free ring, not the v1.0.7 code. Budget for a watchdog/rebuild path and a Developer ID-signed driver.
